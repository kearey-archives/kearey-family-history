V. Collateral Branches and Later Generations (1900 – 1930)

1 Hugh Keary (c. 1824 – 1907)
Occupation and death details: reported in a linked 1907 certificate, not yet inspected in this source audit

“Hugh Keary, farmer, married, died 25 January 1907, aged 83, Durhamstown; informant Christopher Keary, nephew, present at death.”1

The original linked 1907 death image was not included in the audited certificate collection. The stated informant and relationship are an earlier transcription, not an independently reconfirmed finding. No link to Luke of Martry is established.

2 Christopher Keary (1866 – 1945) and Anne (Annie) Keary (1870 – 1943)

Christopher’s birth record (1866) lists Luke Keary and Rose Foley as parents.2 Rose died 1921 aged 82, “Christy Keary son” informant.3 The use of “Christy” for Christopher and the related records suggest a possible identification, but the two references have not been shown conclusively to concern the same man.

Death Records. The cited PDF does not establish Anne Keary’s death in February 1943. A separate register records Christopher Keary’s death in 1945, aged 80, but matching that deceased to the 1866 birth requires corroboration.4 The St Ultan’s burial inscriptions must be considered separately from the civil registrations; burial proximity does not prove parentage or sibling relationships.5

A separate nearby grave (Plot B-2976) records Hugh Keary (1870–1943), while birth registrations cited elsewhere identify a Hugh born in 1870 to James Keary and Bridget (Biddy) Coyle. The relationship between this Hugh, Anne (Annie) Keary and the earlier Durhamstown household requires further verification. A headstone is transcribed as “Erected by Annie Mullen in memory of her uncle Hugh Keary who died 25 Jan 1943 R.I.P.” That wording supports an uncle–niece relationship for the named individuals, but does not by itself establish the proposed first-cousin relationship between everyone discussed here.

Interpretation. The Bohermeen burials and associated records show that the Keary, Foley, Mullen and Martin names recur in the same local setting. The grave inscriptions provide evidence of commemoration and some stated relationships; they do not alone prove the complete cousin network proposed in earlier versions of this study. In particular, the identity of Annie Mullen and her relationship to the Anne (Annie) Keary discussed above should be established before treating their references as interchangeable.

3 Economic and Social Transition (1900 – 1930)

Land reform, livestock markets and changing rural employment shaped County Meath in this period. The records cited on this page do not establish how particular Keary households benefited financially, whether they received grants or when individual holdings passed to other occupiers.
