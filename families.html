VI. Associated Families and Neighbour Networks (1900 – 1930)

1 The Leonard Connection of Ongenstown

The directly inspected marriage register records Luke Keary and Mary Leonard marrying on 1 January 1887:

Marriage: Luke Keary (35), bachelor, farmer, of Martry; father William Keary (farmer). Mary Leonard (20), spinster; father Nicholas Leonard (farmer). Witnesses should be rechecked against the original image before quotation. (Civil Marriage Record, 1887)1

An 1871 Mary Leonard birth page is cited, but an exact matching entry has not yet been securely identified in the source review.2 Her recorded age at marriage is 20. No independent birth year should be inferred from the currently unverified 1871 birth citation.

The 1935 civil entry cited here records Mary Keary as a spinster and names Patrick Keary as her brother; it is not evidence of Mary Leonard’s death.3 Mary’s birth surname Leonard is established by the 1887 marriage; the identity of the Mary in the 1935 death record is separate.

Economic context. Land reforms changed farming in County Meath during the late nineteenth century. The marriage registration does not establish the acreage, wealth or combined property interests of the Leonard and Keary families.

2 Separate Foley records: Kilmessan, Rathfeigh and the Keary birth registration

The 1866 birth registration for Christopher Keary names Luke Keary and Rose Keary formerly Foley as his parents; it does not provide their marriage date. A 1921 death registration records a Rose Keary and names Christy Keary as her son and informant. Identification across the two records requires corroboration before presenting them as a single biography.4

Separately, the 1901 Census of Kilmessan records this Foley household; no relationship to Rose formerly Foley has yet been demonstrated:

Residents of house 18, Kilmessan (Meath)
Francis Foley   45  Head  Labourer
Bridget Foley   38  Wife
William         10  Son
Julia            8  Daughter
Maria            6  Daughter
Mary             4  Daughter
Jane             2  Daughter
Margaret        73  Mother
(Roman Catholic)

(National Archives of Ireland, 1901 Kilmessan return.)5

Jane Foley, aged 2, is recorded as daughter of Francis and Bridget in this 1901 return. A Jane Foley appears as niece in the 1911 Edoxtown household; the identity of the two census individuals has not been independently established.6 A later death registration records a Jane Foley, widow, at Bohermeen on 6 October 1970 and names Kathleen Martin as her niece and informant. The record does not give Jane’s maiden surname or identify her with either census child.7 Kathleen’s recorded relationship to Jane does not establish descent from the Kearys.

3 The Polian and Magrane Households (1911)

The 1911 Census, house 4 in Edoxtown (Rathfeigh), records:

Peter Polian    58  Head of Family  Roman Catholic
Jane Polian     56  Wife            Roman Catholic
Jane Foley      11  Niece           Roman Catholic
James Magrane   74  Uncle           Roman Catholic

The census records Jane Foley as a niece and James Magrane as an uncle in the Polian household. It does not state whether Jane was orphaned, why she resided there, or precisely how the Foley, Polian and Magrane families were related.

4 The Martin Connection and Final Bohermeen Burials

By mid-twentieth century Keary and Foley surnames appeared in the Bohermeen records, but Jane Foley’s maiden name has not been established. Her 1970 death record identifies Kathleen Martin, niece, Slane Road, as informant.7 The cemetery index reports several names at St Ultan’s, Plot B-294, including Anne, Christopher, Thomas and Jane. These inscriptions must be checked individually against civil and burial records before attaching shared parentage or precise dates to the named people.8

Burial in nearby plots is a locational observation, not documentary proof of cousinship, descent or a shared female line.
