The Keary Family of Martry, Durhamstown, and Liscarton, County Meath (c.1730–1978)

A Genealogical and Sociohistorical Study

I. Introduction and Purpose of Study

This study reconstructs the history of the Keary family of County Meath, tracing its principal lines from the mid-eighteenth century to the late twentieth. Drawing upon civil registration, parish records, census returns, valuation office books, and grave transcriptions, it aims to demonstrate not only familial descent but also the social and economic conditions in which successive generations lived.

The Kearys’ story reflects a broader Irish experience: the endurance of rural Catholic families through centuries of penal restrictions, agrarian reform, emigration, and adaptation. From Luke Keary of Martry († 1806) to William Keary of Martry († 1978) — and through Jane Foley (née Keary) of Bohermeen († 1969) — the records span nearly two and a half centuries of family life in County Meath. The closing date reflects the final known Keary burial in Martry, while related families continued locally.

A separate chapter examines Thomas Kearey, baptised in Dublin in 1791, who settled in London around 1816, and later generations of the London Kearey family. A genealogical connection with the County Meath families is possible but has not been established by the evidence presented here. The two histories are therefore considered alongside one another, not as a proven continuous lineage.

The study distinguishes documented findings from plausible family reconstructions and historical interpretation. References point to archival sources and family research where available; some statements still require verification against the underlying records.
