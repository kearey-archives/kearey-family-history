III. The Early Generations: Luke Keary of Martry and His Descendants (c.1730–1857)

1. Luke Keary (c.1731–1806) of Martry
Occupation and religion: not established by the inscription Evidence: Martry gravestone transcription

Luke Keary’s existence is attested by an unusually detailed gravestone in Martry Churchyard:
“Erected by Thomas Keary in memory of their father Luke Keary who departed this life October 24th 1806 aged 75 years. Also his mother Elizabeth Keary alias Rooney who died February 15th 1813 aged 87 years. Also his sister Anne Keary who died April 24th 1800 aged 28 years. Also here lieth the remains of the above Thomas Keary who departed this life 18th January 1857 aged 91 years. Also his beloved wife Anne Keary who departed this life 31st August 1804 aged 82 years.”4

The memorial transcription provides names, stated relationships, dates and ages. It does not, by itself, establish Luke’s occupation, land tenure, religion or literacy. The reported death of Thomas’s wife Anne in 1804, aged 82, is chronologically unusual beside Thomas’s reported age at death in 1857. Both figures are retained as transcribed, not independently confirmed from the stone; the original inscription should be re-examined before interpreting this relationship.

The memorial records Elizabeth Rooney and identifies Thomas (b. c.1766) as Luke’s son. The connection between this Thomas and the separately documented Durhamstown/Liscarton households remains unproven. The wording identifies Thomas as the memorial’s erector; it does not identify his residence or property holdings.

2. Thomas Keary (c.1766–1857) of Martry; Durhamstown identification unresolved
Occupation and tenancy: not established for this individual Spouse recorded on memorial: Anne [surname unknown]

The precise date at which this Thomas Keary, or his family, became associated with Durhamstown is not established by the records cited here. Durhamstown, near Navan, was known for mixed tillage and pasture farming.5 A Griffith’s Valuation entry has been cited for Thomas Keary, reportedly covering a house, offices and 13 acres, 2 roods under the Earl of Essex in Liscarton. The exact original plot and its identification with Thomas of the Martry memorial have not been verified.6

A Valuation Revision Book annotation has been reported as “Dead → to James Keary,” but the exact original entry and plot require inspection. Even if verified, a change in occupier would not, by itself, establish descent from Thomas to James.7 No specific succession is treated as established without inspecting the original revision-book entry.

Historical context (1760–1850). Changes in religious restrictions, land law and the Act of Union (1801) shaped the period. The sources cited here do not establish the memorial family’s landholding, literacy, wealth or particular experience of these developments.

3. O’Hart’s separately reported Durhamstown pedigree
O’Hart’s Irish Pedigrees (1892) gives a Durhamstown pedigree naming Thomas Keary and sons Luke, Hugh and James.8 It also describes a separate account of Martry ancestry. O’Hart is a published secondary pedigree, not independent documentary proof that his Durhamstown Thomas was the Thomas named on the Martry memorial.

  • Hugh Keary: included in O’Hart’s account; later identifications require separate verification.
  • James Keary: a James Keary also appears in the 1796 Flax Growers’ List at Martry; the evidence presented does not identify him with a later James Keary of Durhamstown.
  • Luke Keary: included in O’Hart’s account; not identified as either Luke in the later civil registrations.

These are separately documented Keary households; their placement in a single descendant line from Luke of Martry has not been established.

4. The 1821 Census Households
The 1821 Census of Meath, Ireland’s earliest surviving nominal return, lists multiple Keary families across Martry and Ardbraccan.9 Particularly relevant is the household in Dormstown (Durhamstown):

Thomas Keary, 50  | Head | Farmer
Anne Keary, 35   | Wife | Flax Spinner
Luke Keary, 7    | Son
Thomas Keary, 0  | Son
Anne Keary, 5    | Daughter
Bridget Keary, 2  | Daughter
Jane Clerken, 24  | Servant
Catherine Clerken, 24 | Servant

The identification of this census household with Thomas (son of Luke of Martry) is possible but unresolved. Its Thomas is recorded as aged 50 in 1821, whereas the gravestone places the Thomas who died in 1857 at approximately 55 in that year. More significantly, Anne is recorded as Thomas’s wife in 1821, while the quoted gravestone names a wife Anne who died in 1804. This could reflect remarriage, a separate household or a transcription problem; none is established here. The census records two servants and identifies Anne as a flax spinner. These facts alone cannot establish the household’s wealth or connection to the Martry memorial family.

5. Summary of the Durhamstown Settlement (1730–1857)

The Martry memorial documents Luke and his son Thomas, while the census and later land records document other Keary householders in the surrounding area. Their identification as descendants of the memorial family is unresolved. A reported later valuation annotation names James Keary, but the original entry, plot and identity have not yet been verified. A father-to-son succession cannot be inferred from this report.
