VII. The Meath Census Returns (1821 – 1911) and Family Distribution

1 The 1821 Census as Genealogical Baseline

Four relevant households have been examined in supplied images of original 1821 returns. The original image files are retained in the research collection; stable official record URLs have not yet been documented for every page:

  • House 108, Dormstown (Ardbraccan) — Thomas (50), Anne (35), Luke (7), Thomas (1), Anne (5), Bridget (2), two servants; its precise connection to Luke Keary of Martry has not been proven.
  • House 6, Castlemartin — Thomas (30) and Anne (28), with children Michael (9), Mary (7) and Anne (6).
  • House 12, Castlemartin — John (50) and Mary (48), with sons William (12), Henry (10), Patt (8), Michael (6), John (4) and James (2).
  • House 14, Martry village — John (66) with recorded sons Patt (40), William (30), James (20) and Edward (18), additional daughters, a son-in-law and a grandson; no relationship to the 1806 Luke is established.

These records show Keary households in the same general area; the census entries and gravestone alone do not establish a continuous three-generation pedigree between Martry and Durhamstown.

3 Social and Economic Interpretation (1900 – 1911)

Population context. County Meath’s demographic trends form part of the wider background. The cited household returns do not establish that particular Keary families benefited economically.

Religious life. All censuses record Roman Catholic affiliation and English-speaking literacy, signifying integration within the post-Emancipation Catholic middle strata.

Housing quality. House-and-building returns are required to verify materials and accommodation for each household; the names-and-relationships return alone does not establish building construction or tenure.

Labour and gender. Women (e.g., Anne Keary flax-spinner in 1821) participated in subsistence industry until mechanised linen collapsed by 1890; afterward, domestic service and teaching became common occupations for daughters.

Relationships. Birth, marriage and census records establish the specific people named and relationships recorded in each entry. Wider connections between Keary, Leonard, Foley and Polian families are not assumed without an explicit record.
